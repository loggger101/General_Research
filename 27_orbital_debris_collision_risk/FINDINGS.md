## R135 - First ADR-mission-requirement anchors: six NTRS Liou/Burns papers + one CC BY threshold study (2026-10-07)

Domain 27 had the environment statistics, the institutional cost-benefit values (Colvin/Karcz/Wusk) and the conjunction-assessment chain - but nothing stating **how much** active debris removal is needed. That requirement side is what any future $/object or campaign-cost cell would need as its denominator, so this round registered seven sources for it.

**Six NTRS records pulled and hosted.** All six are NASA-authored (JSC Orbital Debris Program Office / JPL lineage) with copyright.determinationType=GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED and zero in-file copyright statements, so they were committed to `full_texts/` under the R131 ntrs-public-use precedent: Liou 2010 (ISTC Moscow workshop deck - the ~5,500-fragment / >60% LEO-population-increase statement plus the “five objects per year” + [M x Pc] selection criterion), Liou & Johnson 2007 IAC paper (the FIRST quantitative effectiveness table: ERF=36 at 5/yr from 2020, i.e. 935 removals -> -33,337 LEO objects by 2206; cumulative collision-probability reduction 98.7%; diminishing returns to ERF=12 at 20/yr), Burns 2012 (the institutional solution-study abstract: one vehicle “disposing of several large objects per mission”), Liou 2011 engineering-challenges abstract (end-to-end operations chain + target population = spent upper stages, 1 to >8 t, ~10 inclination bands), Liou 2012 remediation paper (cost drivers: multiple ADR systems per launch preferred; tug propellant/ops cost “may be too high for a routine operation”) and Liou 2011 AAS 11-254 (“A Grand Engineering Challenge” - the canonical form of the five-large-objects-per-year statement). Two caveats recorded in access_status: Burns and engineering-challenges are NTRS ABSTRACT-type records (the abstract document IS what NTRS holds), and neither carries an internal date, so their years (2012 / 2011) are assigned from the NTRS accession context and flagged as not stated in-document.

**One CC BY journal paper pulled and hosted.** Yang 2026, Frontiers in Space Technologies 7:1777020 (DOI Crossref-verified; ISSN 2673-5075), carries an explicit Creative Commons Attribution licence statement in file -> `cc-by` token. It supplies the modern counterpoint to NASA’s number: under FCC 5-year deorbit rule + assumed 90% compliance, removal of **~60 large objects (>10 cm) per year** is where debris growth turns negative in the 500-600 km band - with the author’s own caveat that it is “an illustrative threshold under controlled assumptions rather than a robust or universal quantitative value.” The two figures are NOT contradictory: NASA’s ~5/yr targets massive intacts (1 to >8 t) across all LEO altitudes for 200-year stabilization, while Yang’s ~60/yr counts everything >10 cm in one altitude band under a specific compliance scenario. Both are recorded verbatim so any future cell picks deliberately.

**One registered queue row.** Liou et al., “Active Debris Removal mission design in Low Earth Orbit” (EUCASS 4th edn, doi 10.1051/eucass/201304763) - the mission-design half of the anchor set; eucass.eu and doi.org are both bot-walled from this machine and EUCASS proceedings are author-copyrighted per association convention, so it stays `registered_not_pulled` (extraction-only even if pulled).

Key numbers (verbatim) in `extracted_data/r135_adr_requirements_key_numbers.csv`. **No rc opened**: no upstream cell carries an ADR rate or a $/object figure yet - these are additive anchors for d17 insurance-premium drivers, economicspace lifetime/disposal assumptions and any future remediation-cost work.
