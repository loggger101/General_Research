# Domain 28 — Radiation-shielding value of in-space materials

Backings: economicspace `modules/mineral_value.py` `IN_SPACE_UTILITY` and `IN_SPACE_UTILITY_BY_DESTINATION`, the rows whose in-space use is bulk radiation shielding (every silicate and oxide phase, hibonite, and the rock-valued carbonates, oldhamite and phosphates), the shielding part of water's row, and the `"shielding"` default of `_COMMODITY_CLASS`: how much protection a delivered kilogram of each material buys compared with the same mass launched from Earth.

Created in Round 161 (2026-10-13). Blocks are appended one per round, newest last.

## R161 - Domain opened with five NASA shielding reports (2026-10-13; +5 T2, all full_text_hosted)

**Why this domain.** The owner asked for domains covering the utility of each element, compound and alloy in space, so that in-space prices are more accurate. economicspace prices a kilogram delivered to an in-space destination at its utility factor times the launch cost it avoids (`in_space_price_usd_per_kg`), and the utility factors are labelled "ENGINEERING JUDGEMENTS, NOT MEASUREMENTS". Domain 15 holds those tables as market cells (demand, class shares, elasticity). No domain held the engineering question underneath each factor: what a kilogram of the material can do in its role. Round 161 opened three domains split by upstream's own commodity classes: this one (shielding), 29 (structural metals and alloys) and 30 (volatiles, process chemicals and catalyst metals).

**Cells, read at economicspace@8dc759f** (`modules/mineral_value.py`):

| cell | current value | upstream's stated basis |
|---|---|---|
| `IN_SPACE_UTILITY` olivine, pyroxene, orthopyroxene, enstatite, plagioclase, spinel, phyllosilicates, oxides, silicates | 0.25 (base profile: LEO, cislunar, mars_orbit) | "Usable as bulk radiation shielding and as 3-D-printing / sintering feedstock, but a poor per-kg substitute for engineered structure" |

| `_COMMODITY_CLASS` default | `"shielding"` for any unclassified name | an import-time assertion now requires every row to be classed |

**What a source has to supply.** Shielding effectiveness per unit mass of water, silicate rock or regolith, and metal, under one radiation environment and one risk measure, so that the rock rows can be stated as a fraction of water's shielding value; and the same at a planetary surface, where local regolith competes.

**Sources registered (all NTRS, all `GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED`, no copyright statement in any file; hosted under `full_texts/`):**

- `townsend_1990_gcr_shielding_requirements_tm4167` (NASA TM-4167). For the 0.5 Sv/yr blood-forming-organ limit at solar minimum: 3.5 g/cm2 of water, 6.5 g/cm2 of aluminum or 1.0 g/cm2 of liquid hydrogen; at 0.25 Sv/yr the mass ratio is 1:5:11 for LH2:water:aluminum. Aluminum needs 1.86 times the mass of water at the first limit and 2.2 times at the second (computed here).
- `kim_wilson_1994_gcr_shield_materials_performance_tp3473` (NASA TP-3473). A shield performance index that varies by up to a factor of 20 across materials at constant mass, and "the unique role of hydrogenous materials". Its Figs 13 and 15 plot water, liquid hydrogen, lithium hydride, liquid methane, lunar regolith, regolith with 10% epoxy, iron, copper, aluminum and lead on one axis; they are figures only, so no number is taken from them yet.
- `wilson_1997_gcr_spe_shielding_deep_space_tp3682` (NASA TP-3682). To bring the 5-cm-depth dose equivalent at solar minimum to 45 cSv takes about 55 g/cm2 of aluminum but 17 g/cm2 of polyethylene (3.2 times, computed; "approximately a factor of 3" in the text), and aluminum construction used as shielding is "clearly not cost-effective".
- `kim_1998_martian_meteorites_regolith_shield_materials_tp1998_208724` (NASA/TP-1998-208724). Five groups of Martian meteorites give the same dose as the Martian regolith model within 0.5% and the same dose equivalent within 1%; 30 wt% epoxy in the regolith lowers dose equivalent at 50 g/cm2 by 13%. Rock type does not matter; hydrogen does.
- `slaba_mertens_blattnig_2013_radiation_shielding_optimization_mars_tp2013_217983` (NASA/TP-2013-217983). In free space the first 20 g/cm2 of shielding cuts dose equivalent 45-65%, and aluminum-like material gives no significant benefit past about 45 g/cm2. On the Martian surface dose equivalent rises 63% as aluminum goes from 0 to 100 g/cm2, and regolith helps only at several hundred g/cm2.

`lewis_1992_asteroid_resources_sp509_vol3` (registered in domain 29) names "bulk dirt needed for shielding in low Earth orbit" as one of the three asteroid products with an in-space use; it is cited here by id.

**Against the cells.**

- Water: the sources rank hydrogen-rich material first per kilogram, and give aluminum 1.86-2.2 times the shield mass of water for the same limit. Water's 1.00 bundles shielding with three other uses, so this bounds its shielding share without pinning the row.
- Silicate rows (0.25): `kim_1998_martian_meteorites_regolith_shield_materials_tp1998_208724` supports valuing every silicate phase alike, as upstream does. No source here gives silicate or regolith against water per kilogram in text. `kim_wilson_1994_gcr_shield_materials_performance_tp3473` plots it (Fig 13), and digitising that figure is the next step for this domain. The ratio upstream implies (water 1.00 against rock 0.25, a factor of 4) is therefore neither confirmed nor contradicted. It sits above the 1.9-2.2 that water holds over aluminum, so it is consistent with rock being no better than aluminum per kilogram, which nothing registered contradicts.
- `mars_surface` 0.02 and `lunar_surface` 0.03: `slaba_mertens_blattnig_2013_radiation_shielding_optimization_mars_tp2013_217983` finds that on Mars surface aluminum-like mass raises exposure and that regolith is useful only in amounts that need large-scale excavation, and `kim_1998_martian_meteorites_regolith_shield_materials_tp1998_208724` finds imported meteoritic rock equivalent to local regolith. Imported rock buys almost nothing at a surface base; consistent with the overrides.
- `geo` 0.05: `slaba_mertens_blattnig_2013_radiation_shielding_optimization_mars_tp2013_217983`'s free-space curve (most of the benefit in the first 20 g/cm2, none past 45 g/cm2 for aluminum-like material) means a satellite gains little from bulk rock; consistent with the near-zero value.
- Metal rows: `wilson_1997_gcr_spe_shielding_deep_space_tp3682` finds aluminum a poor GCR shield and credits hydrogen content, which metals lack; `kim_wilson_1994_gcr_shield_materials_performance_tp3473` plots iron and copper beside aluminum (Fig 13, figure-only). The structural metal rows (domain 29) should take no credit for shielding, and upstream gives them none.

**No revision candidate.** The cells are labelled judgement, and every finding above bounds or supports them. Extracted data: `extracted_data/r161_shielding_by_material_key_numbers.csv`.

**Still open.** A text-stated (or digitised) per-kg ratio of silicate regolith to water; solar-particle-event shielding by material (the GEO and LEO cases are SPE- and trapped-belt-dominated, not GCR-dominated); and asteroid-specific compositions (CI/CM rock with bound water would shield better than dry silicate).

**Boundary.** Domain 15 keeps the market side of the same tables (`IN_SPACE_ANNUAL_DEMAND_KG`, `_DEMAND_SHARE_BY_CLASS`, elasticity, surplus price) and the economic studies that price delivered material. This domain holds the physics of what a kilogram shields.
## R166 - SPE shielding at a surface site + water-in-regolith enhancement (2026-10-14; +2 T2, both full_text_hosted)

**Why these.** The R161 opening block left three items open: a per-kg silicate-to-water ratio in text, 'solar-particle-event shielding by material (the GEO and LEO cases are SPE- and trapped-belt-dominated, not GCR-dominated)', and asteroid-specific compositions with bound water. This round closes the second at a surface site - the destination where local regolith is actually used as shield mass - plus the first measured number on how much of the polar ice finding matters for shielding.

**Sources registered (both NTRS public-use, hosted under `full_texts/`):**

- `nealy_wilson_townsend_1989_solar_flare_shielding_regolith_lunar_base_tp2869` (NASA TP-2869, Langley Research Center; microfilm-era scan with a garbled OCR text layer - verbatim quotes taken from NTRS's machine-extracted fulltext, which agrees count==1 with the PDF layer on every span). BRYNTRN transport-code doses for three large historical flares (Feb-1956, Nov-1960, Aug-1972) in slab / cylinder / half-buried-sphere regolith configurations: 'for 50 cm of regolith shield, the computed dose is well below 1 rein, which would be considered nonhazardous' (Aug-1972; unshielded it 'is at or near the lethal dose (_ 500 rem at 5-cm tissue depth)'); 'the maximum interior dose is slightly in excess of 9 rem for the 1956 solar flare' (half-buried sphere, 50-cm top shield - its high-energy component stays above 10 rem even at 50 cm); design conclusion 'While 50 cm of regolith thickness may provide adequate flare protection, larger thicknesses may be more desirable'. Lunar-soil model: density as-printed from the OCR layer ('the soil mass density was assumed to be 1.5 g/cm 3, based on the density range of 0.8 to 2.15 g/cm 3 reported in reference 7'); BFO limits at 5-cm depth 'The specified maximum levels at this 5-cm depth are 25 rein/month and 50 rem/year' (rein = OCR of rem).
- `johnson_gersey_wilkins_zhou_2011_radiation_shielding_regolith_polyethylene_water_ntrs20110012713` (NTRS abstract record, single page; no venue field in the NTRS metadata - year 2011 from accession/publicationDate context per the R138 lesson). TEPC measurements behind regolith/UHMWPE/water mixture panels at a proton synchrotron and LANSCE: 'adding 2 wt% water to regolith particles could increase shielding of the regolith materials by about 6%', motivated by LCROSS's finding that polar regolith carries 'up to 5.6 +/-2.8 weight percent (wt%)' water.

**Against the cells.**

- `lunar_surface` rock rows (0.03) and imported-rock value: TP-2869 is exactly the institutional record that at a surface site the shielding material IS the local regolith - 'the use of lunar soil as a shielding material is attractive in that shielding materials need not be transported to the lunar base'. A delivered kilogram of rock buys nothing there (local mass does the job for free), which is precisely what the 0.03 override states; consistent, no re-pin.
- `lunar_surface` water row (0.60): Johnson's ~+6% per 2 wt% is small against dry regolith but it is the only measured number tying LCROSS's polar ice to shielding performance - supports bundling a shielding credit into water's lunar-surface utility without pinning the factor.
- `geo` rock row (0.05): unchanged in kind - TP-2869's GCR side ('relatively large thicknesses (_ 10 cm) are required' for an acceptable annual level, computed on aluminum-like shields) says bulk mass still has to be carried to orbit; nothing here contradicts the near-zero value.
- The per-kg silicate-to-water text ratio remains open (TP-2869 gives dose-vs-thickness curves in figures only, as TP-3473 does); digitising one of them is still this domain's next step.

**No revision candidate.** The cells are labelled judgement; both findings bound or support the overrides without contradicting any value. Extracted data: `extracted_data/r166_spe_shielding_key_numbers.csv` (9 rows x 6 cols).

## R179 - Methodological foundation + mission-scale shield-mass requirement via the TM-4444 reference list (2026-10-16; +2 T2 full_text_hosted)

**Why these.** The R178 round registered TM-4444 and codified the companion-paper technique. RP-1257 is the transport-methods monograph behind every LaRC shielding calculation in this domain; TM-4422 gives the first institutional mission-scale shield-mass requirement - how much extra mass radiobiological uncertainty demands for a lunar mission.

**Sources registered (both NTRS GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED, zero in-file copyright on full-page scans; hosted under `full_texts/`):**

- `wilson_townsend_et_al_1991_transport_methods_interactions_space_radiations_rp1257` (NASA RP-1257, 620 pp, Dec-1991; Wilson & Townsend LaRC + Schimmerling LBNL/UC Berkeley + Khandelwal/Khan ODU + Nealy/Cucinotta/Simonsen/Shinn LaRC + Norbury Rider College). Preface scope: 'This report presents a brief history leading to the involvement of the Langley Research Center of the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) in space-radiation physics and protection. Indeed, a relatively complete summary of technical capability as of the summer of 1990 is given.' [clean span]. Boltzmann equations for coupled ionic/neutronic fields with inversion techniques - the method base of BRYNTRN/HZETRN that generated every dose curve registered in this domain (TP-2869, TP-3473, TM-4167).
- `wilson_nealy_schimmerling_1993_radiobiological_uncertainty_shield_design_tm4422` (NASA TM-4422, 12 pp, Feb-1993; Wilson & Nealy LaRC + Schimmerling USRA Washington DC). NOTE: the reference list of TM-4444 prints this as 'TM-,I,122' - the number was verified from the document itself (p.1 prints NASA Technical Memorandum 4422) per the R178 lesson. Abstract: 'Quantitative estimates of shield mass requirements as a function of a radiobiological uncertainty factor are given for a simplified vehicle structure. The additional shield mass required for compensation is calculated as a function of the uncertainty in galactic cosmic ray exposure, and this mass is found to be as large as a factor of 3 for a lunar transfer vehicle.' Safety-factor quantification: 'an estimate for the value of S is 3, which corresponds to an effective dose equivalent that is 300 percent greater than one obtained from currently accepted dosimetric analyses'; risk predictions 'accurate only within an order of magnitude (factor of 10)' with a design consequence quoted as-printed around OCR artifacts ('de- signed' / 'de-. sign'): shielding designed for below a 3-percent excess cancer risk may in reality be designed for 30 percent. Mission profile: two configurations - '(1) a complete spherical shell representing a lunar transfer vehicle (LTV) in cis-lunar space and (2) a hemispherical shell representing a habitat on the lunar surface' [joined across hyphen linebreaks; OCR prints 'hemispher- ical'] over 'a mission duration of 60 days: 45 days on the lunar surface, 5 days of transit time each way, and 5 days in low Earth orbit' [same]. Table I LEO limits (OCR): BFO 0.25 Sv per 30 days / 0.50 Sv annual ('I' = OCR of 1). Cost context: payload mass cost 'about 30000 dollars/lb or 66 million dollars metric ton (tonne)' for a human mission (1993 dollars; OCR prints hmar).

**Against the cells.** Bounds class at mission scale. The x3 additional-mass factor applies to GCR-compensation shielding on a lunar transfer vehicle - it bounds how much of habitat/depot structural mass could be radiation-driven, without pinning any per-kg utility value; consistent with water 1.00 / silicate 0.25 rows where the shielding credit is one of several utilities. The ~$30k/lb (~$66M/metric ton) figure is a 1993 institutional cost context for lunar-mission payload - same order as upstream launch-cost anchors, no re-pin warranted from it. RP-1257 pins nothing numerically; its role is provenance: every transport-code dose in this domain traces to these methods.

**Near-misses (recorded per R174 convention):** refs 4 and 6 of TM-4444 - 'Effects of Biological Uncertainty on Deep Space Mission Shield Design' (NASA TP-3312, 1993) and 'Fully Energy Dependent FZETRN: A Galactic Cosmic-Ray Transport Code' (NASA TP-3243, 1992) - are absent from NTRS under any title variant tried this round; if they surface in an institutional pull later they would extend the same class.
