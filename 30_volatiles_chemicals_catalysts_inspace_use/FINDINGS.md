Backings: economicspace `modules/mineral_value.py` `IN_SPACE_UTILITY` and `IN_SPACE_UTILITY_BY_DESTINATION`, the rows in `_COMMODITY_CLASS` classes `"propellant"` (water, ammonia), `"chemical"` (carbon, organics, carbon dioxide, carbonates, sulfur, phosphorus, oldhamite, merrillite, chlorapatite) and `"trace"` (the PGMs and their ores, gold, silver, gallium, germanium, rhenium, tungsten, molybdenum), and the comment that records why PGMs get no catalyst utility at the surface destinations: what each volatile, process chemical and trace metal is used for in space and how well it replaces the launched alternative.

**What a source has to supply.** Performance of each volatile as propellant or life-support consumable against the launched alternative (Isp, storability, processing steps); the mass of catalyst and process chemical an ISRU plant or habitat consumes; and evidence on in-space uses of the trace metals.

**Sources registered (all NTRS public-use, hosted under `full_texts/`):**

- `degroot_1997_electrolysis_propulsion_spacecraft_tm113157` (NASA TM-113157 / AIAA 97-2948, `GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED`). Water electrolysed on board and burned as GH2/GO2 has "performance measured at an Isp of over 350 s", 355 s on a 22-N thruster over 69,000 firings, projected above 380 s with radiation-cooled chambers, "superior to earth storable chemical alternatives"; water stores at 1.0 g/cc in light low-pressure tanks. Stoichiometric GH2/GO2 may need "iridium-coated rhenium" chambers.
- `morren_1987_multipropellant_resistojet_space_station_tm100113` (NASA TM-100113 / AIAA 87-2120, `GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED`; the file declares itself a work of the U.S. Government not subject to copyright). The space-station resistojet was baselined to provide drag make-up "while disposing of a variety of fluids expected to be present in excess quantities on board", measured on hydrogen, helium, methane, steam, nitrogen, air, argon and carbon dioxide at Isp 93-385 s; steam reached 184 s with a separate boiler and 159 s with the thruster as boiler.
- `franco_2019_sabatier_catalyst_mars_isru_ices2019_077` (ICES-2019-077, NASA KSC, `PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED`). Sabatier methanation for a Mars ISRU plant uses "transition metal catalysts (such as ruthenium or nickel)"; the tests compared a nickel catalyst with 0.5% and 2% Ru coatings on alumina, for a plant sized at 8.03 kg CH4/day (6978 kg over the campaign).

`lewis_1992_asteroid_resources_sp509_vol3` (domain 29) gives C1 chondrites up to 20% bound water, 6% organic matter and 11% magnetite, with nitrogen, sulfur and carbonates present; cited here by id.

**Against the cells.**

- Water 1.00: one kilogram of delivered water is at least as good as one kilogram of launched storable propellant (`degroot_1997_electrolysis_propulsion_spacecraft_tm113157`), and stores more easily than cryogens. Supports the base row as a drop-in substitute; the destination overrides are about local supply and are outside these sources.
- Carbon dioxide 0.30 and organics 0.20: `morren_1987_multipropellant_resistojet_space_station_tm100113` documents a direct low-Isp propellant use (resistojet drag make-up) for CO2 and methane on top of the Sabatier feed use upstream cites; a non-zero value is supported, its size is not.
- Ammonia 0.60: no source yet. `morren_1987_multipropellant_resistojet_space_station_tm100113`'s fluids do not include ammonia; the resistojet and nitrogen make-up uses upstream cites still need an anchor.
- Trace class 0.00 and the rejected 0.05: `franco_2019_sabatier_catalyst_mars_isru_ices2019_077` shows that the catalyst use is real but a percent-level Ru coating on an alumina support, with nickel as the substitute, which supports upstream's kilogram-scale catalyst market (trace share 0.0005 in domain 15) and its reasoning for dropping the 0.05. `degroot_1997_electrolysis_propulsion_spacecraft_tm113157`'s Ir/Re chambers are a second real in-space use of PGM-group and refractory metals, but as parts made on Earth.

**No revision candidate.** Extracted data: `extracted_data/r161_volatiles_chemicals_catalysts_key_numbers.csv`.

**Still open.** Ammonia and nitrogen consumption by habitats (ECLSS make-up rates) and ammonia arcjet/resistojet performance; catalyst inventories of ECLSS (ISS Sabatier, oxygen generation) and of fuel cells, in kg of PGM per system; sulfur and phosphorus demand by in-space processing and agriculture.

## R168 - Ammonia arcjet performance class + ISS Sabatier catalyst record (2026-10-14; +3 T2, all full_text_hosted)

**Why these.** The R161 opening block left three items open: ammonia and nitrogen consumption by habitats (ECLSS make-up rates) plus ammonia arcjet/resistojet performance; catalyst inventories of ECLSS in kg of PGM per system; sulfur and phosphorus demand. This round closes the ammonia-performance half with two NTRS-hosted institutional records - NASA's 30-kW-class ammonia arcjet program (JPL) and a low-power DC arcjet technical memorandum on hydrogen/nitrogen/ammonia mixtures (LeRC) - and registers the ISS Sabatier catalyst-poisoning investigation as the first ECLSS-catalyst record. Near-miss candidates documented per convention: NTRS 19850057477 (Miyake & McKevitt, 'Performance characterization tests of a 1-kW resistojet using hydrogen, nitrogen and ammonia') and 19860052336 (Zafran, 'Resistojet operation with various propellants') are both metadata-only records (determinationType=OTHER, downloadsAvailable=false) - not registered; the two hostable arcjet papers cover the performance class.

**Sources registered (all NTRS GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED, zero in-file copyright per pymupdf scan of every page plus the .txt layers, hosted under `full_texts/`):**

- `deininger_et_al_1990_30kw_ammonia_arcjet_jpl_pub90_4` (JPL Publication 90-4, 'The 30-kW Ammonia Arcjet Technology Final Report', program July 1986 - December 1989; NTRS 19900015874) = the institutional record of NASA's high-thrust ammonia arcjet. Corrected performance: "Since the 30-kW ammonia arcjet is now known to produce less than 800 s specific impulse and 30-percent thrust efficiency, further development is necessary before the technology can be broadly applied." (p.41). Measurement-correction record: "The error caused JPL mass flow measurements to be 20-percent low, leading to calculations of specific impulse and thrust efficiency which were 20-percent high." (p.37) - previously published values for this engine are ~20% too optimistic. Program scope quoted as two sub-spans because the abstract sentence is OCR-garbled in both text layers: 'a 30-kW class ammonia-propellant arcjet' and 'extended over 3 1/2 years' (p.3).
- `hardy_curran_1987_low_power_dc_arcjet_hydrogen_nitrogen_ammonia_mixtures_tm89876` (NASA TM-89876, AIAA Paper 87-1948; venue verified on p.1: 'Prepared for the 23rd Joint Propulsion Conference cosponsored by the AIAA, SAE, ASME, and ASEE San Diego, California, June 29-July 2, 1987') = low-thrust-class ammonia arcjet data: "The arcjet had a typical performance of 450 sec specific impulse at 1 kW with hydrogen/nitrogen mixtures." (p.25); "As expected, the ammonia mixtures gave specific impulse levels approximately 10 sec higher than the hydrazine mixtures at a given power-to-mass flow rate." and "At each point the specific impulse was greater than 400 sec and there was a variatlon of 40 to 80 sec Isp between the highest and lowest current level at each mass flow rate." (p.9, 'variatlon' as printed). Lifetime context quoted from NTRS's machine-extracted .txt endpoint because the PDF text layer garbles the sentence opening: "According to this study, this arcjet had an expected lifetime of less than 2 hr with ammonia." (p.3; refers to a prior 1-kW-class thruster cited in ref. 5 - 920 W input, 550 s Isp).
- `nallette_et_al_2013_sabatier_catalyst_poisoning_investigation` (NTRS single-page abstract record, UTAS + MSFC; NTRS 20140002591) = the institutional ECLSS-catalyst record: "The Carbon Dioxide Reduction Assembly (CRA) on the International Space Station (ISS) has been operational since 2010." and "Currently, there is no provision to remove contaminants prior to entering the Sabatier catalyst bed." - trace organics concentrated by the CDRA's 5A molecular sieve are a documented poisoning risk for the in-flight ISS Sabatier reactor. Year from NTRS publicationDate (2013-07-14), not stated in document.

## R169 - Institutional habitat nitrogen-demand figures (2026-10-14; +1 T2 full_text_hosted)

**Why this.** The R168 block closed d30's ammonia-performance half and left 'ammonia and nitrogen consumption by habitats' open. This round registers the institutional answer to its N2 side: a NASA JSC / Amentum trade study (ICES-2026) that evaluated exactly how much nitrogen every currently planned future vehicle/habitat consumes per mission, from Orion through long-term Mars habitation - the demand-side figures behind upstream's ammonia-row basis ('nitrogen for a habitat'). The study covers N2 supply specifically; ammonia as a feedstock is not among its options (N2O and N2H4 decomposition are), so it anchors the nitrogen-consumption half, not an ammonia inventory.

**Source registered (NTRS PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED, zero in-file copyright per pymupdf scan of all 15 pages; hosted under `full_texts/`):**

- `masangkay_sweterlitsch_frahlman_2026_supplying_nitrogen_moon_mars_ices2026_238` (ICES-2026-238, 55th International Conference on Environmental Systems, Rio Grande PR; venue line verified on p.1) = the nitrogen supply trade study across all currently known future missions (~Artemis 6 to long-term Mars habitation). Institutional definition of use: "Nitrogen is primarily used to maintain cabin pressure in vehicles/habitats and is lost nominally to the environment through leaks, airlock usage, and technology venting." (p.1) - N2 is not a metabolic consumable, so demand scales with EVA frequency and mission duration rather than crew count. Full requirement range: "N2 requirements provided/estimated range from around 9 kg to upwards of 4,600 kg" (p.7). Table 4 per-mission ranges as printed on p.13: transit 9-295 kg / lunar surface 19-3623 kg / Mars surface 119-4620 kg. Airlock concept of operations is an order-of-magnitude driver (Table 5, p.14): "As can be seen in Table 5, the Mars surface habitat N2 usage range differs by an order of magnitude when air save is used versus when it is not." - air save 119-729 kg vs no air save 1092-4620 kg. Supply-side results: all non-compressed-gas options (supercritical N2, cryogenic flux capacitor, liquid N2/N2O/N2H4) trade better than compressed gas "for N2 requirements greater than ~124 kg of N2" (p.13); the study recommends pursuing liquid N2 with power and liquid N2O with power for development (p.14). Martian-atmosphere ISRU sizing basis: the lowest Mars surface requirement is 119 kg, "corresponding to ~412,000,000 L of Martian atmosphere at the assumed conditions" (210 K / 6 mbar, 3% N2; p.7-p.8 page-boundary split quoted as two sub-spans).

**Against the cells.**
- Ammonia 0.60 (base): the habitat-side basis ('nitrogen for a habitat') now has institutional per-mission demand figures - transit missions stay under ~300 kg, lunar surface up to ~3.6 t, Mars surface 119-4620 kg depending on air save. These are mission-level totals (a context/bound class), not a per-crew ECLSS make-up rate, and they bound the nitrogen that an ammonia-derived supply chain would have to replace; no re-pin.
- Water / trace rows: unaffected - the study's N2O-decomposition option notes O2 byproduct benefit but registers no water or catalyst figures used here.

**No revision candidate.** Extracted data: `extracted_data/r169_habitat_nitrogen_demand_key_numbers.csv` (9 rows x 6 cols). Companion record NTRS 20260005329 is the presentation deck of this same study - not separately registered. Still open from R168's list: catalyst inventories in kg of PGM per system; sulfur and phosphorus demand.
