## R86 - First empirical launch-reliability anchors (2026-09-30; registry 311 -> 313)

Two NTRS-hosted conference presentations, both pulled live from ntrs.nasa.gov this round and hosted under `full_texts/` (NTRS copyright determination PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED for each - same precedent as the R82 d3 rows):

- **cross_vesely_2018_lv_first_flight_failure_probability** [T2] - PSAM 2018 (JSC): builds a database of ALL US+foreign launches 1980-2017 by model type, then computes an assumed new-vehicle design from every failure on the first two flights of each model -> **first-flight failure probability = 0.134 (success ~0.866)**; with assurance-program credit for heritage elements (equivalent of 5 flights: solid propulsion, upper-stage engines, TVC) the total drops to **0.0898 (~success 0.910**). Element rates per element-launch: upper-stage liquid engines 2.03E-02 (highest), avionics 1.32E-02, stage separation 1.36E-02, TVC 1.95E-03 (lowest). Conclusion p11 verbatim: new vehicles have 'significantly higher average failure probability than mature launch vehicles' and PRA 'do[es] not adequately assess their failure probability'.
- **al_hassan_novack_2015_bayesian_reliability_data_applicability** [T2] - Huntsville SRE RAM VIII 2015 (MSFC): the METHOD for exactly this domain's small-sample problem. A new vehicle is heritage + new hardware; its failure rate is estimated with lognormal priors per reliability block, each weighted by a subjective data-APPLICABILITY heuristic over generic sources (NPRD/EPRD/NUCLARR, MIL-HDBK-217F part-count), then correlated and Monte-Carlo'd. 'Higher data applicability improves certainty of estimates' (p16).

## R90 - First empirical failure-rate dataset + first measured learning-curve anchor (2026-09-30; registry 317 -> 319)

**jacklin_2019_small_satellite_mission_failure_rates** [T2, full_text_hosted] - Stephen A. Jacklin (NASA Ames), "Small-Satellite Mission Failure Rates", NASA/TM—2018–220034 (March 2019; NTRS GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED). **The first empirical failure-rate dataset in d16** - the brief's 'on-orbit failure or survival analyses of spacecraft that fit a lifetime distribution' bullet, at fleet level. **chen_mclennan_2004_learning_curve_impact_three_identical_spacecraft** [T2, full_text_hosted] - Guangming Chen (Morgan State) & Douglas D. McLennan (GSFC ST-5 PM), NTRS GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED. **The first MEASURED learning-curve anchor in d16** for the 'measured learning-curve slopes for spacecraft production runs' bullet: fits an exponential improvement model (d(Ct-C0)/dt = -k(Ct-C0) -> constant PERCENTAGE reduction per unit, converging to floor C0; p8-p9) to the actual recurring-cost data of three identical 20-kg-class ST-5 spacecraft built at Goddard (Figures 4/5), and derives the standard conclusions empirically - resources per unit decrease with repetition, improvement rate diminishes as more units are produced ('net reduction for 2nd unit from the first should be larger than ... for 3rd'), constant floor C0 once skills reach sufficient level (p8). ## R93 - First lifetime-distribution fits for the MTBF cell + ISS PRA Bayesian failure-rate derivation (2026-10-01; +3 rows -> registry 327)

The d16 brief's core unbacked requirement: "On-orbit failure or survival analyses of spacecraft that fit a lifetime distribution, to test both the exponential form and the 30-year scale for deep-space craft." This round registers three sources - two hosted, one open-not-pulled - that are exactly such fits.

- **Navard 1993** (NASA/ASEE Summer Faculty Fellowship final report at JSC; T2, full text hosted - NTRS GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED): the first registered source to FIT a lifetime distribution to real spacecraft field data and test it. The Heydorn et al. mixture-of-exponentials model (each component's failure rate k drawn from a gamma posterior under a uniform prior, so individual hazard rates are constant but the population hazard is decreasing) was fitted for best fit to Kaplan-Meier empirical survival functions of electronic assemblies from 28 satellites launched since 1976 (the PRC/Bloomquist et al. dataset, originally over 300 satellites). The operating-time sample is heavily censored - 165 observations with only 14 failures and the 82 largest times truncated - which is precisely why a nonparametric product-limit estimator was needed. CONCLUSION (p.8, verbatim): "a mixture of exponential distributions does not adequately explain electronic failures seen in previously flown satellites. In particular, it does not model the early failures very well." Implication for d16: upstream's `Spacecraft mean time between failures` cell assumes pure-exponential survival P = exp(-T/MTBF) - this study finds that form (even its decreasing-hazard mixture generalization) inadequate where spacecraft electronics actually fail, i.e. in early life; the 30-year scale itself is not tested by this source (pre-1985 data).
- **Vitali & Lutomski 2004** (PAM conference paper, Berlin; T2, full text hosted - NTRS PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED): institutional Bayesian derivation of component-level failure rates for the ISS PRA. Two-phase updating: Phase I builds lognormal prior distributions from MADS + RAC NPRD/EPRD evidence with Poisson likelihoods (ReDat software); Phase II updates them with actual ISS on-orbit failures from the PRACA OOF database. Table 2 gives the resulting probability-of-failure per ORU category for six months of operation - Electronics mean 6.10E-03, Electrical 7.00E-03, Electro-Mechanical 2.50E-02, Mechanical 2.80E-02 (each with 5th/95th percentiles; read off a page render because the NTRS text layer garbles the table). This is d16's "treat small samples explicitly" requirement in practice - every rate carries a distribution and an error factor, not a point estimate.
- **Fox et al. 2013** (IEEE Aerospace Conference; T2 per the repo's R70 convention that conference papers are institutionally reviewed rather than peer-reviewed journal articles, open_not_pulled): THE direct source for the MTBF cell - Weibull-vs-exponential lifetime analysis on the ratio of mission operating lifetime to design life across "a large, publically available dataset" (first such study per its abstract), Kaplan-Meier survivor function estimated from operating lifetimes, bootstrap resampling for uncertainty. NTRS record 20150008736 exists but carries NO downloadable file (downloads=[], copyright determinationType=OTHER) and the IEEE publisher copy is not accessible from this machine; DOI 10.1109/aero.2013.6497352 Crossref-verified live. Abstract recorded verbatim in FINDINGS below; re-class + extract when a full text is obtained (institutional repository or author copy).

No revision candidate: none of the three contradicts an upstream value - Navard's fit rejection concerns the FORM assumption (already flagged as "no failure dataset" by upstream), and Fox et al.'s fitted values are not yet readable. The MTBF cell stays unbacked until Fox et al. is pulled; its row now records exactly what a pull must supply. Extracted data: `extracted_data/r93_lifetime_distribution_fits_key_numbers.csv` (12 rows).

**Fox et al. 2013 abstract (verbatim from the NTRS record):** "Estimating the operational lifetime of satellites and spacecraft is a complex process. Operational lifetime can differ from mission design lifetime for a variety of reasons. Unexpected mortality can occur due to human errors in design and fabrication, to human errors in launch and operations, to random anomalies of hardware and software or even satellite function degradation or technology change, leading to unrealized economic or mission return. This study focuses on data collection of public information using, for the first time, a large, publically available dataset, and preliminary analysis of satellite lifetimes, both operational lifetime and design lifetime. The objective of this study is the illustration of the relationship of design life to actual lifetime for some representative classes of satellites and spacecraft. First, a Weibull and Exponential lifetime analysis comparison is performed on the ratio of mission operating lifetime to design life, accounting for terminated and ongoing missions. Next a Kaplan-Meier survivor function, standard practice for clinical trials analysis, is estimated from operating lifetime. Bootstrap resampling is used to provide uncertainty estimates of selected survival probabilities. This study highlights the need for more detailed databases and engineering reliability models of satellite lifetime that include satellite systems and subsystems, operations procedures and environmental characteristics to support the design of complex, multi-generation, long-lived space systems in Earth orbit."
## R94 - First measured regolith-abrasion wear data + first used-equipment resale source (2026-10-01; +4 rows -> registry 331)

The d16 brief's last two unbacked requirements: "Wear-life data for mechanisms working in abrasive regolith (seals, bearings, cutting surfaces), and, as a terrestrial analogue for salvage, the residual value of used heavy mining equipment." This round registers four sources - one hosted, three extraction-only.

- **Kunneparambil Sukumaran et al. 2026** [T2, full text hosted; NTRS GOV_PERMITTED, no in-file copyright]: THE direct source for the mechanism-wear cell - actual joint mechanisms (rod-slot, ball-socket and hinge assemblies in Al6061 and Ti6Al4V) abraded with JSC-1A lunar regolith simulant under vacuum, VPS Ti-2vol% hBN coated vs uncoated. Conclusion verbatim: "over 80% reduction in wear depth compared to uncoated components under abrasion from JSC-1A lunar regolith simulant", while "uncoated specimens exhibited irregular force responses, severe abrasion, and regolith-induced gouging". Honest limitation (p5): the joint geometries did not allow reliable CoF/wear-rate measurement - so this is a relative, qualitative-to-semi-quantitative result. It does NOT give hours-to-failure for any rig component; it shows which components fail first (uncoated joints) and that coating mitigation is an order of magnitude on wear depth.
- **Sukumaran et al. 2024** [T1 peer-reviewed, Surface & Coatings Technology 492:131185; extraction-only - in-file "copyright 2024 Elsevier B.V. All rights are reserved" outranks NTRS GOV_PERMITTED per the licence rule]: sliding-wear rates of Ti-hBN coatings with and without JSC-1A third-body abrasion (900 HV simulant that fractures into sharp-edged particles). Conclusion verbatim: "all exposed samples exhibit an impressive 90-95 % reduction in wear rate compared to the Ti CM substrates"; regolith present -> "significantly higher wear rate ... compared to scenarios without regolith". Same first-author group as the AMS paper (FIU Plasma Forming Lab) - this is its journal version with full quantitative methods.
- **Stein et al. 2025** [T2, ICACC conference deck; extraction-only - NTRS MAY_INCLUDE_COPYRIGHT_MATERIAL + no in-file licence]: Taber abrasion per ASTM D4060-19 comparing standard CS-17 carbide wheels against LMS-1 regolith-simulant wheels: bare Al6061-T6 wear index 3.29 (CS-17) vs 3.64 mg/1000 cycles (LMS-1); APS alumina coatings 5.19-5.39; 8YSZ 9.20 - "8YSZ coating abrades nearly twice as much as the alumina coating samples". - **Mas 2007** [T2 working paper, NBER WP w13138; extraction-only - in-file "copyright 2007 by Alexandre Mas. All rights reserved"; no journal version on Crossref]: the brief's terrestrial salvage analogue at last - resale prices and six-point condition ratings for >48,000 used construction machines sold at auction Jan 1994-Sep 2002 (Equipment Watch data). Its central result: a quality/wear shock moves used-equipment resale prices by ~5% after regression adjustment. Caveat recorded in the row: this is US construction equipment in a LIQUID market with many buyers - upstream's own note says an asteroid-parked rig has "a market of approximately one buyer", so 0.5 of remaining book value stays unbacked as a pin; Mas supplies the structure (condition-indexed depreciation) any salvage model should take, not its level.

## R95 - Mechanism success rates by type: the opening brief's last item closes (2026-10-01; +4 rows -> registry 335)

- **Shapiro et al. 1995, Vol I Summary** [T2, full text hosted; NTRS PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED]: NASA-TM-107046 (Lewis/MTI under contract NAS3-27086) - the fleet-level mechanism-reliability study.
