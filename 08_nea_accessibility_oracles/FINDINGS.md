# Domain 8 — NEA accessibility oracles (R53)

**Scope.** This domain registers the two EXTERNAL delta-v oracles that economicspace's `probe_nhats.py` (FINDINGS F4/F6) uses to validate its shipped one-way estimator against work built by other people. The reachability question — *which near-Earth asteroids are cheap enough to mine* — is the core economic driver of the whole pipeline, and these two services are the ground truth it is measured against. Both were pulled live from this machine on 2026-09-24 (not just titles) and their key statistics extracted to `extracted_data/r53_nea_oracle_key_numbers.csv`.

**1. NASA/JPL NHATS** (`ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/nhats.api`) — T3, live service. JPL's own numerically optimised **round-trip** delta-v for every NEA it can find a viable crewed trajectory for: real ephemerides, multi-revolution transfers, entry-speed capped; one HTTP call, no kernels or solver. Pulled this round at N=7092 bodies (the repo's probe reported 7,033 at its run date — the service is live and refreshing). Round-trip dv **median 9.367 km/s** (p10 5.99, p90 11.55; range 3.39-12.00), which **reproduces the repo's own SECOND-PASS.md F6 figure to 3 decimal places** — independent confirmation that we are reading the service correctly. Distribution: [0-5) km/s: 281 (4.0%); [5-8) km/s: 1879 (26.5%); [8-10) km/s: 2114 (29.8%); [10-99) km/s: 2818 (39.7%). Population is bounded (JPL lists only bodies with a viable crewed trajectory), so this is an accessibility ladder, not a census.

**2. Asterank NEO service** (`www.asterank.com/api/asterank`, MIT) — T3, live service. The standard published **Shoemaker-Helin closed-form** delta-v carried as the `dv` column over ~600k bodies (1,000-row NEA-filtered sample pulled this round). S-H dv **median 8.140 km/s** (p10 6.15, p90 14.44) — again reproducing the repo's F6 figure to 3dp. Note: Asterank **carries** the closed form already registered in d3 as `shoemaker_helin_1978`; this row is the live service, not the 1978 paper. Its economic columns (profit/price) return degenerate values through the public endpoint (99/200 nonzero), confirming the repo's documented 'use dv only' caveat.

**Anchor scope — honesty note.** Both oracles are **round-trip** quantities; our `delta_v_segments.csv` NEA rows (easy 4500 / average 6500 / hard 8500 m/s) are mostly **one-way legs**. So these services anchor the ladder as a *distribution and rank* reference, not exact cell pins — exactly the distinction economicspace's own probe makes ('what transfers is the rank disagreement and its sign, not the kilometres per second'). The one clean cross-check: our 'easy NEA' tier maps to NHATS bodies with round-trip dv < 5 km/s = 4.0% of the accessible population (the ultra-low class Elvis et al. 2011, already registered in d3 as `elvis2011`, identifies as ~65/6699 known NEOs). The median-NEA round trip (9.4 km/s) sits above our average one-way (6.5 km/s) + a powered return, consistent with the ladder's shape.

**What this closes.** The reachability/accessibility question now has two registered, live, independently-pulled institutional oracles behind it — JPL's numerically optimised trajectories and the standard analytic approximation. This is criterion #1 domain-gap work (d8) and directly serves 'items we can actually access and analyze': both services returned full data this round.

## R113 - The NHATS filter, and a mining-specific accessibility database (2026-10-04; +2 sources, T2x1, T1x1)

From the owner's link list.

**`barbee_mink_adamo_2011_nhats_methodology`** (hosted, NTRS public use) defines the filter that `jpl_nhats_nea_dv_oracle` applies: Earth departure 2015-2040, round trip <=450 days, stay >=8 days, departure C3 <=60 km2/s2, total delta-v <=12 km/s including departure from a 400 km circular parking orbit, entry speed <=12 km/s, and size >=30 m (H <=26.5 at an assumed albedo of 0.05). Phase II: of 7,665 NEAs in SBDB on 2011-02-03, 765 passed the trajectory filter with 79,157,604 trajectory solutions, and 590 also passed the size cut. The oracle's round-trip delta-v therefore always carries these limits; its 12 km/s ceiling and 450-day duration are the constraints to state when its median is compared with economicspace's one-way legs.

**`xie_bennett_dempster_2021_nea_mining_target_evaluation`** (registered; abstract read via Semantic Scholar; Elsevier paywall; the volume is 181, not 178 as the list had it) builds a database of 6,373 accessible NEA mining missions of up to 6 years in 2030-2065, finds that ~55% of accessible NEAs allow a stay of more than 4 years in a 6-year mission, and estimates retrievable water, PGM and silicate mass per asteroid under C and non-C assumptions. It is the closest published counterpart to economicspace's ranking and the next oracle to pull.

Extracted data: `extracted_data/r113_nhats_methodology_key_numbers.csv` (4 rows).

## R138 - NASA's own definition of "accessible NEO": the NHATS criteria deck (2026-10-09)

Domain 8 had the live oracles (JPL NHATS API, Asterank Shoemaker-Helin column), the 2011 methodology paper and a mining-target T1 - but no source stating what "NHATS-compliant" actually MEANS as criteria. NTRS topic search for 'near earth object accessibility assessment' surfaced Barbee's SBAG-12 presentation (NASA GSFC, Jan 7 2015), NTRS 20150000798 - GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED with zero in-file copyright statements -> hosted under ntrs-public-use.

The deck is the institutional statement of the filter behind jpl_nhats_nea_dv_oracle, verbatim (slide 3): Earth departure date between 2015-01-01 and 2040-12-31; Earth departure C3 ≤60 km²/s²; total mission Δv ≤12 km/s - where the total includes "(1) the Earth departure maneuver from a 400 km altitude circular parking orbit, (2) the maneuver to match the NEA's velocity at arrival, (3) the maneuver to depart the NEA and, (4) if necessary, a maneuver to control the atmospheric re-entry speed during Earth return"; total round trip mission duration ≤450 days; stay time at the NEA ≥8 days; Earth atmospheric entry speed ≤12 km/s at an altitude of 125 km. "A near-Earth asteroid (NEA) that offers at least one trajectory solution meeting those criteria is classified as NHATS-compliant."

Population and calibration (slides 5-6, verbatim): "As of 2014-12-06, 1317 NHATS-compliant NEAs have been discovered" - of which 49 are visitable+returnable for less Δv than lunar orbit and 556 for less than the lunar surface; all 1317 more accessible than Mars/Phobos/Deimos. The calibration table (slide 5) gives round-trip Δv/duration pairs for other destinations - Mars Surface 12.53 km/s / 923 d, elliptical Mars orbit 6.29 / 923 and 12.14 / 422 (stay-time extremes), Venus-flyby variants 12.81 / 485 and 8.12 / 588, Mars flyby 9.01 / 501 and 6.07 / 582 - closing with the deck's punchline: "No round-trip mission to Mars (orbit, surface, or flyby) or Phobos/Deimos is possible with both Δv ≤12 km/s AND mission duration ≤450 days." That sentence is what makes the NHATS thresholds a meaningful accessibility LADDER rather than arbitrary numbers.

Match quality: this pins the DEFINITION of d8's oracle output (the criteria are now citable from an institutional source instead of reverse-engineered), and gives a second dated population point (1317 @ 2014-12-06) bracketing our live scans (N=7092 R53 / N=7094 R73). No upstream cell pinned - the criteria match what probe_nhats.py already consumes.
